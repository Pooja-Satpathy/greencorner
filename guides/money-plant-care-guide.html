How to Care for a Money Plant

The money plant is one of India's most popular houseplants — and one of the easiest. Here's how to keep yours lush, full and growing, whether it lives in soil or a jar of water.

GCBy GreenCorner Editorial TeamPublished 12 October 2026 · Updated 7 November 20267 min read

Which plant is the "money plant"?

In India, "money plant" almost always means golden pothos (Epipremnum aureum), a trailing vine with heart-shaped green leaves splashed with yellow. The name is also used for other plants — the jade plant (Crassula ovata) and the Chinese money plant (Pilea peperomioides) — which need quite different care. This guide covers golden pothos.

Light

Bright, indirect light gives the fastest growth and the strongest variegation — a spot a metre or two from an east- or west-facing window works well. Money plants tolerate medium and low light, which is why they're so common in offices, but growth slows, the gaps between leaves get longer and the yellow markings may fade. Avoid strong afternoon sun through glass, which can scorch the leaves.

Watering

Water when the top 2–3 cm of soil feels dry. In a warm Indian summer that's often about once a week; in winter it may be every 10–14 days. Water thoroughly until it drains from the bottom, then empty the saucer.

  • Too much water: yellowing lower leaves, soft stems and soil that stays wet for days.
  • Too little water: limp, drooping leaves and dry, crispy edges. The plant usually perks up within a day of watering.

See how often to water indoor plants for a step-by-step soil check.

Soil and pots

Use a light, well-draining potting mix — a ready-made organic potting mix or a blend of cocopeat, compost and perlite works well. Always choose a pot with a drainage hole. Money plants don't need a large pot; move up just one size (about 2–5 cm wider) when roots circle the pot or poke out of the drainage hole.

Feeding

Feed every 4–6 weeks during the warm growing months with a diluted, balanced liquid fertiliser such as liquid seaweed plant food. Skip feeding in winter, when growth slows. Too much fertiliser can burn roots and cause brown leaf tips, so it's better to under-feed than over-feed.

Pruning for a fuller plant

Long, bare vines are common, especially in lower light. Pruning encourages bushier growth:

  1. Use clean, sharp pruning shears or scissors.
  2. Cut just above a node — the small bump where a leaf joins the stem.
  3. New shoots usually grow from the node just below the cut.
  4. Root the cuttings (see below) and plant them back into the same pot for extra fullness.

Propagating money plant in water

  1. Take a cutting 10–15 cm long with at least two or three nodes.
  2. Remove the lowest leaf so a node can sit under water.
  3. Place it in a clean jar of water in bright, indirect light, keeping the leaves above the waterline.
  4. Change the water every week. Roots usually appear within two to four weeks.
  5. Once roots are a few centimetres long, plant the cutting in soil or keep growing it in water.

Growing permanently in water? Refresh the water weekly, rinse the jar to prevent algae, and add a few drops of diluted liquid fertiliser about once a month — plain water contains very few nutrients.

Common problems and fixes

Money plant symptoms, likely causes and fixes
SymptomLikely causeWhat to do
Yellow lower leavesOverwatering or poor drainageLet the soil dry more between waterings; check the pot has a drainage hole.
Brown, crispy edgesUnderwatering, very dry air or direct sunWater more consistently and move away from harsh sun.
Long vines with few leavesNot enough lightMove closer to a window and prune to encourage branching.
Fading variegationLow lightGive brighter, indirect light.
White, cottony spotsMealybugsIsolate the plant, wipe pests off with a cotton bud dipped in rubbing alcohol and repeat weekly.

Is the money plant safe for pets?

No. The ASPCA lists pothos as toxic to cats and dogs; chewing the leaves can irritate the mouth and stomach. Hang it out of reach — a macramé plant hanger works well — or choose a pet-friendly alternative such as a spider plant.
