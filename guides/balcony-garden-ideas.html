How to Create a Balcony Garden

Even a narrow balcony can grow flowers, herbs and vegetables. Follow these steps to plan a balcony garden that suits your sunlight, space and schedule.

GCBy GreenCorner Editorial TeamPublished 5 November 2026 · Updated 7 November 20269 min read

Step 1: Measure your sunlight

Sunlight decides what will grow. On a clear day, note when direct sun reaches the balcony and when it leaves:

  • Full sun (6+ hours): ideal for flowering plants, fruiting vegetables and most herbs.
  • Partial sun (3–6 hours): leafy greens, many herbs and some flowering plants.
  • Shade (under 3 hours): foliage plants such as money plant, snake plant and ZZ plant.

Sun angles change through the year, so a balcony that's sunny in winter may be shaded in summer. Re-check when the seasons change.

Step 2: Check weight, drainage and safety

  • Wet soil is heavy. If you're planning large planters or raised beds, check load limits with your building management first.
  • Keep the floor drain clear and use saucers so water doesn't drip onto neighbours below.
  • Secure everything. Railing planters and hanging pots must be firmly fixed, especially on windy, high floors.
  • Check society rules about railing planters, grills and water use.

Step 3: Plan your layout

Think in layers so you use every surface:

  • Floor: larger pots for shrubs like hibiscus in the sunniest corners.
  • Railing: railing planters for herbs, marigolds and trailing plants.
  • Walls: shelves, or a trellis for climbers like bougainvillea and mogra.
  • Above: hanging planters for trailing plants in shadier spots.

Leave space for a chair. Enjoying the garden is the whole point.

Step 4: Choose containers

As a rough guide, herbs grow well in pots 15–20 cm deep, most flowering annuals in 20–25 cm, and tomatoes, chillies and brinjal in 30 cm or more. Root vegetables like radish and carrot need at least 20–30 cm. Whatever you choose, it must have drainage holes — like our terracotta pot set.

Step 5: Use a good potting mix

Container plants need a light, well-draining mix that still holds moisture. A common homemade blend is roughly equal parts garden soil, cocopeat and compost such as vermicompost — adjust if it stays soggy. A ready-to-use organic potting mix saves time. Refresh the top few centimetres with compost each season.

Step 6: Pick plants for your light

Full sun (6+ hours)

Hibiscus, bougainvillea, roses, mogra and marigolds; vegetables such as tomato, chilli and brinjal; and herbs like tulsi and basil.

Partial sun (3–6 hours)

Coriander, mint, methi and palak, plus foliage plants like spider plants and areca palms in bright shade.

Shade (under 3 hours)

Money plant, snake plant and ZZ plant — kept sheltered from heavy monsoon rain and harsh afternoon sun.

Seasonal planting: in much of north and central India, the cooler months (roughly October–February) suit marigolds, petunias and calendula, and vegetables like palak, radish, carrot, peas and tomato. In milder southern climates, the timing differs. Check a local sowing calendar, then try marigold seeds or a winter vegetable seed combo.

Step 7: Set up a watering routine

  • Balcony pots dry out faster than garden beds. In summer, small pots in full sun may need water every day.
  • Water early in the morning or in the evening, when less is lost to evaporation.
  • Add a layer of mulch — dry leaves or coir — to keep soil cooler and moister.
  • Group pots together so they shade each other, and use a long-spout watering can to reach the soil.
  • Going away? Move pots out of direct sun, group them and water deeply before you leave — or ask a neighbour.

Step 8: Maintain and enjoy

Feed flowering plants every two to four weeks during the growing season, remove faded flowers to encourage more, prune leggy growth and check for pests weekly. Log watering and feeding in the free Plant Care Planner so nothing gets missed.

A simple balcony starter kit

  • Two or three railing planters or 6–8 inch pots with drainage holes
  • Potting mix and a bag of vermicompost
  • Three easy plants to start: tulsi, marigolds and a money plant for the shadier corner
  • A watering can and a basic gardening tool kit
