Best Plants for Small Apartments

A small flat can still feel lush. The trick is choosing plants that grow up or down instead of out, and making the most of every bright windowsill, shelf and railing.

GCBy GreenCorner Editorial TeamPublished 26 October 2026 · Updated 7 November 20266 min read

Start with your light, not your floor space

In a compact home, light is usually the real limit. Spend a day noticing how the sun moves through your rooms:

  • East-facing windows get gentle morning sun — ideal for most houseplants.
  • West-facing windows get strong afternoon sun; keep sensitive plants a little back from the glass.
  • South-facing windows receive the most light through the day in India and the rest of the northern hemisphere, especially in winter — great for succulents and herbs.
  • North-facing windows give soft, indirect light — best for low-light tolerant plants.

Not sure what your space supports? The Plant Finder recommends plants based on your light, care preference and available space.

Compact plants that don't need much floor space

Snake plant

It grows up rather than out, so even a tall plant fits beside a sofa or in a narrow corner. It tolerates low light and needs watering only every few weeks. View the snake plant.

ZZ plant

Glossy, structured leaves that look tidy on a side table or desk. It handles low light and dry spells, and grows slowly, so it won't outgrow its spot quickly. View the ZZ plant.

Zebra haworthia and small succulents

Perfect for desks and windowsills. Zebra haworthia tolerates bright, indirect light, while echeveria and jade prefer a sunny sill with some direct sun. A succulent trio makes an instant mini display.

Peace lily

A medium-sized plant that brings white flowers to rooms without direct sun. Choose a compact plant for a bedside table or shelf. View the peace lily.

Hanging and trailing plants

When floor and shelf space runs out, look up. Trailing plants in hanging planters soften corners and keep leaves out of reach of pets and children.

  • Money plant — fast-growing and forgiving; trails beautifully from a high shelf. See our money plant care guide.
  • Spider plant — arching leaves and baby plantlets; listed as non-toxic to cats and dogs by the ASPCA.

Check that your ceiling hook or bracket can hold the weight of a freshly watered pot, and use a saucer or cover pot to catch drips. A cotton macramé hanger is a simple option.

A windowsill kitchen garden

A sunny windowsill or balcony railing with 4–6 hours of direct sun can grow tulsi, basil and coriander. Herbs grown indoors without direct sun tend to become leggy, so give them your brightest spot. Start with a kitchen herb seed kit or a potted tulsi plant.

Space-saving ideas

  • Go vertical: tiered plant stands and wall shelves hold several plants in the footprint of one.
  • Use the railing: balcony railing planters turn an unused edge into a herb or flower box — fix them securely.
  • Group plants on a tray: clustering makes watering quicker and looks intentional.
  • Choose lightweight pots: plastic or fibre nursery pots inside decorative covers are easy to move towards the light in winter.
  • Edit regularly: a few healthy plants look better than many struggling ones.

Small-space mistakes to avoid

  • Putting plants in dark corners just because there's room there — choose the plant for the light, not the gap.
  • Using pots without drainage holes to protect floors — use a cover pot and saucer instead.
  • Overcrowding shelves: poor air flow can encourage pests and fungal problems.

For more pitfalls, read 10 common gardening mistakes. If you have a balcony, our step-by-step balcony garden guide will help you plan it.
